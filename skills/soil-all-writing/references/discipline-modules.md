# Discipline modules

Load only the modules needed for the text. Combine modules explicitly when research is interdisciplinary.

## D1 Pedology and soil resources

Soil genesis, morphology, micromorphology, horizons, classification, geography, survey, mapping, paleopedology, land evaluation, soil resource inventory, pedometrics, digital soil mapping. Record the classification system and edition before editing names.

## D2 Soil physical processes

Texture, structure, aggregation, porosity, bulk density, compaction, water retention, infiltration, conductivity, preferential flow, aeration, gas transport, heat, mechanics, shrink–swell, erosion and sediment transport. Protect quantity definitions, boundary conditions, and measurement bases.

## D3 Soil chemistry and mineralogy

Acidity, alkalinity, salinity, sodicity, redox, sorption, exchange, colloids, interfaces, clay minerals, oxides, weathering, speciation, complexation, kinetics, thermodynamics, organic matter chemistry. Distinguish operationally defined pools from chemical species.

## D4 Fertility and plant nutrition

Fertility, nutrient availability, plant nutrition, rhizosphere, fertilizers, amendments, nutrient-use efficiency, deficiency/toxicity, soil–plant systems and nutrient management. Do not treat “available”, “extractable”, “exchangeable”, and “bioavailable” as synonyms.

## D5 Soil biology and ecology

Microbiology, microbial ecology, fauna, fungi, enzymes, roots, decomposition, food webs, biodiversity, community assembly, functional traits, molecular ecology, genomics, metagenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics and bioinformatics. Protect taxonomic formatting and distinguish abundance, activity, potential, and function.

## D6 Biogeochemistry and global change

Carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus, sulfur and metal cycles; greenhouse gases; isotopes; priming; stabilization; mineral-associated and particulate organic matter; climate warming, drought, deposition and land-use change. Preserve pool definitions, flux/stock distinctions, time scale and isotope notation.

## D7 Pollution, risk and remediation

Metals/metalloids, pesticides, petroleum, persistent organic pollutants, antibiotics, resistance genes, microplastics and emerging contaminants; transport, exposure, ecotoxicology, ecological and human-health risk, remediation and monitoring. Distinguish concentration, fraction, dose, exposure, hazard and risk.

## D8 Degradation, conservation and restoration

Erosion, compaction, acidification, salinization, sodification, desertification, contamination, sealing, degradation assessment, soil and water conservation, reclamation, restoration and ecosystem services. Name the baseline, reference condition, indicators and spatial/temporal scale.

## D9 Ecosystem and land-use contexts

Cropland, paddy, forest, grassland, wetland, urban, industrial, mining, permafrost, arid, saline, tropical, alpine, coastal and anthropogenic soils. Do not transfer a term or mechanism across contexts without checking its operational meaning.

## D10 Earth and environmental sciences

Geology, geomorphology, mineralogy, sedimentology, Quaternary science, geochemistry, hydrology, hydrogeology, climatology, atmospheric deposition, Earth-system science, environmental chemistry, environmental microbiology and ecosystem ecology.

## D11 Agriculture and resource management

Agronomy, crop science, tillage, agroecology, horticulture, forestry, grassland science, irrigation and drainage, nonpoint pollution, agricultural waste recycling, conservation agriculture, land management and life-cycle assessment.

## D12 Measurement, statistics and computation

Sampling, experimental design, analytical chemistry, spectroscopy, chromatography–mass spectrometry, XRD, XAS, microscopy, NMR, isotope methods, sequencing, incubation, field and long-term experiments, biostatistics, spatial statistics, geostatistics, meta-analysis, uncertainty, process models, reactive transport, remote sensing, GIS, proximal sensing, hyperspectral/UAV methods, machine learning and reproducibility.

## D13 Optional adjacent fields

Geotechnical and environmental engineering, soil mechanics, public health, food safety, toxicology, standards and conformity assessment, policy and resource economics. Apply the adjacent field’s governing terminology and do not let the soil-science module override legal or engineering definitions.

## Cross-module ambiguity examples

- `soil organic matter` and `soil organic carbon` are related but not interchangeable.
- `content`, `concentration`, `stock`, `storage`, and `sequestration` encode different quantities or processes.
- `turnover`, `residence time`, `mean age`, and `persistence` require model and time-scale context.
- `available phosphorus` usually means an operational extraction, not a universal plant-available pool.
- `saline`, `sodic`, and `saline-sodic` depend on defined criteria and system.
- `amendment`, `conditioner`, `fertilizer`, and `remediation agent` carry different functional and sometimes regulatory meanings.
- `microbial biomass`, `abundance`, `activity`, `functional potential`, and `process rate` must remain distinct.
- `prediction`, `mapping`, `interpolation`, `classification`, and `causal inference` are not stylistic substitutes.
